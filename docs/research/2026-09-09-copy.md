## 2. Idea catalog (copy mechanisms, not visual ones — 20+)

1. **Benefit-laddering ("so that…") chains** — Name a feature, then chain "so that" once or
   twice until it lands on an outcome the reader already wants (Julian Shapiro's core drill).
   *Maxfolio fit*: Shopify Work index lead — "Shopify Functions price the bundle at checkout,
   so the discount always matches inventory, so nobody ships a $0 order by mistake."
   *Sketch*: plain prose, no component change — a copy edit to the existing lead paragraph.
   *A11y/perf*: none (text only).

2. **Specificity swap (adjective → number/noun)** — Marketingexamples' most repeated lesson:
   replace "fast," "reliable," "modern" with the concrete fact that adjective is standing in
   for. *Fit*: hero lead, manifesto band, FAQ answers — anywhere "robust engineering" appears,
   swap for "800+ automated tests" or "2 checkout-level Shopify Functions."
   *Sketch*: copy-only pass with a checklist (grep the site copy for adjective list: fast,
   robust, modern, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class — replace each).

3. **"Who it's for" in sentence one** (Fletch PMM pattern, general knowledge) — State the
   reader's identity before the pitch: "For Shopify merchants whose store already has traffic."
   *Fit*: hero eyebrow or the first clause of the hero headline.
   *Sketch*: text only; keep under ~8 words so it survives 390px without wrapping oddly.

4. **Outcome-before-feature stacking** — Order every section as outcome (H2) → mechanism
   (one sentence) → proof (a number), never feature-name → outcome.
   *Fit*: Process stepper captions, Shopify Work case-sheet openers.
   *Sketch*: reorder existing captions; no new markup.

5. **Mirror-the-customer's-own-words** (Copyhackers doctrine, general knowledge — the site's
   captured 2026 homepage confirms the house voice still practices this: "Some people love AI
   slop" mirrors how a skeptical buyer actually talks, not a category label) — Draw the
   headline's problem language from how a merchant actually complains ("my store gets traffic
   but nobody buys"), not internal jargon ("conversion rate optimization services").
   *Fit*: hero headline candidates below lean on this directly.
   *Sketch*: none needed beyond copywriting; optionally A/B one jargon vs one plain-language
   headline using the existing Vercel split-test infra.

6. **The 4 U's headline check** (Useful, Urgent, Ultra-specific, Unique — Copyhackers,
   general knowledge; the 4 U's framework itself was not restated verbatim on the captured
   homepage, so this specific naming is still general knowledge, flagged accordingly) — Score
   any headline candidate against all four before shipping it; a headline strong on only one or
   two U's underperforms.
   *Fit*: used as the scoring rubric for the 25 headlines below.

7. **One-screenshot-one-lesson micro-format** (marketingexamples.com house style) — Each
   FAQ answer or manifesto line does ONE job and stops; no compound sentences stacking two
   claims. *Fit*: FAQ answers, manifesto band. *Sketch*: cap FAQ answers at 2 sentences.

8. **Loss-aversion framing over gain framing** (marketingexamples.com copywriting archive,
   Digitdeck's own house doctrine per `digitdeck-copywriting` skill) — "Stop losing orders at
   checkout" tests stronger than "Get more orders." *Fit*: contact section headline, CTA
   reassurance lines. *Sketch*: pair one loss-framed headline with one gain-framed variant for
   the A/B test cell already planned.

9. **Negative-framing contrast pairs** ("Not an agency retainer. Not a fixed test count." —
   seen directly in the existing LENS 3 KNR/Convertibles capture, reused here as a copy
   mechanism) — Rule out the reader's feared alternative before naming the real offer.
   *Fit*: hero identity pill, or directly under the free-review CTA.

10. **Button copy that carries the offer, not a verb** — "Get your free 20-minute store
    review" beats "Get Started" because the button IS the offer (HEY pattern, already logged
    in LENS 3, restated here as a CTA-label rule applied across all 12 candidates below).

11. **Reassurance line directly under every CTA, not just the primary one** (HEY/Basecamp,
    LENS 3) — extended in this pass to a formula: reassurance = [what you will NOT get] +
    [what you WILL get] in under 12 words. *Sketch*: `<span class="reassurance">` under each
    `<button>`, `font-size: 0.8em`, `opacity: .7`, no motion.

12. **Freelancer-marketplace trust phrasing** ("Vetted", "Top X%", "background-checked") —
    from Toptal/Storetasker-style marketplace copy (general knowledge of the category; direct
    capture pending per the incomplete-run note). *Fit for Max*: he isn't marketplace-vetted,
    so the honest analogue is naming the real gate — "18+ live storefronts a paying client
    approved," not an invented badge. Flag: do NOT borrow the vetted-badge language literally;
    borrow only the structural idea of "state the proof gate up front."

13. **Response-time commitment as trust copy** — Freelance-profile pages almost always state a
    reply SLA ("responds within 24 hours"). *Fit*: contact section promise line — Max's real
    fact is "within one business day," already used in LENS 3's copy block; this pass confirms
    it as a recurring pattern worth keeping, not dropping.

14. **Eyebrow-as-audience-filter** — A one-line eyebrow that self-selects the right reader
    ("For Shopify merchants, not agencies shopping for a subcontractor") rather than a role
    title alone. *Fit*: alternate hero eyebrow variant for the A/B test.

15. **The "diagnosis, not a pitch" reframe** — Position the free 20-minute review as a
    diagnostic act (find the leak) rather than a sales call (hear the pitch) — directly
    supported by the loss-aversion doctrine and by Julian Shapiro's "reduce perceived risk of
    the first click" chapter. *Fit*: every CTA reassurance line in this deck uses this frame.

16. **FAQ-as-objection-preemption, ordered by objection severity** — Order FAQ entries from
    the objection most likely to kill the click (price/cost) to the least (tech stack
    curiosity), per standard landing-page-copy sequencing (Shapiro). *Fit*: reorder existing
    FAQ; no new component.

17. **Manifesto lines as short, declarative, present-tense claims, never future-tense promises**
    ("I ship measured" not "I will ship measured work") — marketingexamples.com's tone rule
    for confidence copy. *Fit*: the 6 manifesto lines below all use present tense.

18. **Section eyebrows as one-line previews, not labels** (extends LENS 3's Basecamp
    nav-as-teaser finding into eyebrow copy specifically) — an eyebrow like "Shopify Work"
    becomes "18 stores, all with real checkouts" so the eyebrow itself carries information.

19. **Numeral-first subheads on mobile, adjective-first on desktop** (LENS 3's Superhuman
    finding, reapplied as a copy rule for the 15 subheads below — each subhead below is
    written numeral-first so it already works at 390px without a mobile-only rewrite).

20. **CTA label parallelism across all three CTAs** (review / see-work / download-CV) — each
    CTA label uses the same verb-tense and length band (4–6 words) so the row reads as one
    system, per the pattern implied by growth.design's twin-CTA row in LENS 3.

21. **"Sample data" disclosure folded into the sentence, not a footnote** — instead of a
    separate asterisk, the reassurance line itself says "Sample metrics are always labeled" —
    already partly present in LENS 3's copy block; this pass extends it to two more CTA
    variants below so the honesty rule is systemic, not attached to one button only.

22. **The "Purchase Rate = Desire − (Labor + Confusion)" litmus test** (confirmed capture,
    julian.com) — Shapiro's actual working formula: every hero element should either raise
    desire or cut labor/confusion, never add either. *Fit*: use this literally as the review
    checklist for the hero rewrite — if a clause doesn't do one of the three jobs, cut it.
    *Sketch*: none needed — a copy-review heuristic, not a component.

23. **The "read only the header" self-identification test** (confirmed capture, julian.com) —
    Shapiro's litmus test: if a visitor reads ONLY the H1, do they know exactly what's sold?
    His own before/after pairs (e.g. "Improve your workflow!" → "Visually design and develop
    sites from scratch. No coding.") show the fix is always added specificity, never a punchier
    adjective. *Fit*: run each of the 25 headlines below through this test explicitly — several
    (e.g. #6, #14) are intentionally identity-forward and should be paired with a subhead that
    passes the test on its own, since the headline alone doesn't.

24. **Marketplace "acceptance-rate" trust stat** (confirmed capture, storetasker.com: "reviewed
    over 15,000 … less than 5% are asked to join") — the mechanism is stating a rejection
    funnel, not a badge. *Fit for Max*: the honest analogue isn't a rejection rate (he isn't a
    marketplace), but the same mechanism applies to his real funnel — "18 stores shipped out of
    every client Digitdeck has taken on" is a defensible, real version of the same structure.
    Flag: only ship this if the ratio is real and checkable; do not approximate it.

25. **Bio-copy order: years → scale-proof noun → named prior context** (confirmed capture,
    toptal.com profile bios: "22 years of experience… recently engineered a 250+ section custom
    ecosystem… replacing high-fee SaaS with proprietary code") — every Toptal bio leads with a
    tenure number, then one concrete built-thing, then the business outcome, in that fixed
    order. *Fit*: rewrite maxfolio's own bio-length copy (About/CV blurb) into this three-slot
    order instead of a prose paragraph: tenure → concrete built-thing → business outcome.

26. **Two-tier skills split (Hard Skills / Soft Skills)** (confirmed capture, storetasker.com)
    — separates "impressive work experience, e-commerce expertise, technical skills" from
    "communication, project management, reliability" as two named, parallel lists. *Fit*:
    maxfolio's Skills usage ledger could add a second, shorter column doing this same split so
    "800+ tests, 2 Functions" (hard) sits beside "one business-day response, EN/ES/JA" (soft) —
    answers the objection that a technical portfolio only proves code, not collaboration.

27. **Self-diagnostic checklist before the offer** (confirmed capture, fletchpmm.com: "Do you
    have a positioning problem? (Check all that apply)" — 5 checkbox-style symptom statements
    directly under the headline, before pricing) — Let the visitor self-qualify by ticking
    which symptoms match, rather than telling them they have the problem. *Fit for maxfolio*: a
    small interactive checklist above the Contact section — "Is your store leaking revenue?
    Traffic's fine but orders lag. / Cart abandons past a certain price point. / You've never
    seen the Function that prices your bundles." — answers double as the visitor building their
    own case for booking the free review, no invented stat needed since each line is a real,
    generic Shopify symptom Max already diagnoses. *Sketch*: plain checkboxes, `localStorage`
    only for the checked state (no server write), Lighthouse-safe.
    *A11y*: native `<input type="checkbox">` + `<label>`, no custom widget needed.

28. **AI-anxiety contrast headline** (confirmed capture, copyhackers.com 2026 homepage: "Some
    people love AI slop. For everyone else, there's Copyhackers.") — Name the reader's current
    fear (AI-generated, interchangeable work) and position the offer as the human alternative,
    in two short sentences, no paragraph of justification. *Fit*: an optional hero variant or
    FAQ entry for maxfolio addressing "did an AI build this portfolio/these stores?" honestly —
    "Every section here started as a Figma or Framer reference a human approved, then got built
    by hand" — matches the project's own no-invented-content rule while using the same
    contrast-pair mechanic. *Sketch*: text-only FAQ addition, no new component.

29. **JA business-register CTA convention** — Japanese B2B CTAs conventionally state a
    concrete next step and a time-bound response ("ご相談は無料です" + a business-day figure)
    rather than an English-style imperative alone; applied to the JA renderings below (general
    knowledge of JA B2B UX convention — flag for native review before shipping, per Max's
    honesty rule, since no JA-market site was captured this pass).

## 3. Avoid list

- Buzzword stacks with no picturable noun ("leverage synergies," "holistic solutions,"
  "end-to-end transformation") — confirmed again by marketingexamples.com's own running
  commentary, which mocks this pattern repeatedly in its archive.
- Headlines that describe the *company* ("Digitdeck is a Shopify agency") instead of the
  *reader's* situation — Shapiro's handbook flags this as the single most common landing-page
  headline mistake.
- CTA verbs with no object ("Learn More," "Get Started") — weak on all 4 U's; every button in
  this deck names the specific thing delivered.
- Borrowing marketplace trust badges (Toptal "Top 3%," Storetasker "Vetted Expert") verbatim
  when the underlying vetting process doesn't exist for Max — this is exactly the invented-
  credential risk the project's absolute-honesty rule forbids; use only the *structural* idea
  (state a real proof gate), never the badge language itself.
- Compound, two-claim sentences in FAQ/manifesto copy — marketingexamples.com's whole format
  bets against this; every deliverable below is capped near one claim per sentence.
- Literal word-for-word translation of EN CTA reassurance lines into ES/JA — confirmed again
  as a risk in LENS 3's copy block; the ES/JA rows below are native renderings, not
  translations, and are marked for a native speaker's review before shipping (no JA-market
  site was captured this pass to validate register).

### 8 FAQ answer phrasing patterns (with a worked example each)

1. **Lead with the number, not the hedge.** "How many stores have you actually shipped?" → "18, live right now — click any of them above." `[specificity]`
2. **State the boundary before the capability.** "Do you build from scratch?" → "Only when nothing existing fits. Most stores need the leak found, not a rebuild." `[negative-framing]`
3. **Answer the price question with a process, not a dodge.** "What does the review cost?" → "Nothing. 20 minutes, one call, a written list after." `[honesty + specificity]`
4. **Name the real gate, never a borrowed badge.** "Are you vetted by Shopify?" → "I'm a working Shopify Tech Lead — the stores on this page are the credential." `[avoid-borrowed-trust-badge, own the real proof]`
5. **Convert a technical answer into an outcome sentence.** "What are Shopify Functions?" → "Checkout-level code, so a bundle's price is always correct — two of my stores run on it live." `[outcome-before-feature]`
6. **Preempt the objection before it's asked.** "Is this a sales call?" → "No — it's the diagnosis. The pitch only happens if you ask for one." `[diagnosis-not-pitch]`
7. **Answer language questions with the fact, not an adjective.** "Do you work in Spanish?" → "Yes — EN, ES, and JA are all live on this site, not just offered." `[specificity]`
8. **Close every answer with the next real step, not a generic CTA.** End each FAQ answer with the actual next action ("Book the free review" / "See the store it's live on") rather than a repeated "Contact us." `[CTA parallelism]`
